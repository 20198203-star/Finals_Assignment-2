ABOUT TEXAS

Texas is nicknamed the Lone Star State because in 1836, when the Republic of Texas declared itself an independent nation, it flew a flag with a single star on it.

MUSIC

Even the entertainment is bigger in Texas. From the up-and-comers to the tried-and-true talent, Texas marks the spot for attention-grabbing artists, directors and musicians. Snag some popcorn for groundbreaking productions, iconic short films or the latest studio releases at one of Texas’ film festivals. Or find a new live music venue or music festival where you can jam out to your new favorite artist. Whatever you’re looking for, you’ll find it with a trip to Texas.

Local Music Venues Across the State

Combining history with boot-scooting, Gruene Hall near San Antonio is said to be the oldest continually operating dance hall in Texas. Built in 1878, the building still wears its historic character with a tin roof, big dance floor, and a modest stage where musicians from many genres hold court.

In West Texas, Marfa’s artistic community thrives around Ballroom Marfa, a performance space that’s a creative hub for visitors from around the world. This venue attracts musical performers, both local and international, and complements its musical offerings with film and visual arts.

Texas Music Festivals

Every October, Austin’s Zilker Park transforms into a music lover’s paradise during the Austin City Limits (ACL) Festival. This iconic two-weekend event draws fans from around the globe to catch world-class headliners and emerging artists spanning rock, pop, hip-hop, and country. Beyond the music, ACL showcases Austin’s best—local eats, craft brews, and family-friendly zone dubbed “Austin Kiddie Limits.” Set against Austin’s ever-changing skyline, ACL Fest captures the essence of why Austin is celebrated as the Live Music Capital of the World.

For 18 days each spring, the Kerrville Folk Festival weaves music into the natural beauty of Quiet Valley Ranch. This festival draws devotees of folk and Americana to a place where these genres are alive and well. Camp under the stars, join a workshop, or relax to the acoustic sounds floating about. With its laid-back vibe and strong sense of togetherness, Kerrville is a beloved gathering for those who cherish the roots of American music.

Turn Up the Live Music in Houston

Houston, Texas, is a big place with an enviable variety of music offerings and venues available for all kinds of music-lovers to discover for themselves. Here are just a few of the city’s notable spots.

The Rustic

For a laid-back, Texas-style music experience, The Rustic is a go-to destination. Combining live music, farm-to-table food, and a spacious outdoor setting, this venue offers an unbeatable atmosphere for enjoying both well-known and emerging artists. With a strong emphasis on country, folk, and Americana, The Rustic provides an intimate yet energetic space where guests can kick back with a craft beer, savor Southern-inspired dishes, and take in live performances under the stars. Whether you're in the mood for a casual night out or a lively weekend show, The Rustic delivers a true Houston music experience.

The Heights Theater

Nestled in the heart of Houston’s historic Heights neighborhood, the Heights Theater offers a charming, vintage atmosphere for live performances. Originally built in the 1930s, the theater has been lovingly restored, maintaining its old-school character while hosting a variety of performances. From intimate acoustic sets to electrifying rock shows, the Heights Theater draws music lovers with its unique blend of modern sound and nostalgic ambiance. With a rich history and an inviting environment, this venue is a must-visit for anyone looking to experience the cultural heartbeat of Houston.

Things to Do in Texas

Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex

Looking for fun things to do with kids? The newly opened Meow Wolf Grapevine is full of imaginative, interactive surprises that have landed the large-scale, immersive art installation on numerous “best of” lists. Dubbed The Unreal Real, Meow Wolf Grapevine is never the same for any two visitors thanks to its many rooms to experience, doors to open, and stories to follow. The Metroplex’s many stellar museums include the Dallas Museum of Art, a cool oasis with traveling art shows as well as exhibits from the museum’s 25,000-piece collection that spans 5,000 years.

Epic Waters Indoor Waterpark, Grand Prairie

TEpic Waters is a year-round waterpark with a massive indoor collection of rides and attractions, as well as a summer-season wave pool. Challenge your friends on the E-Racer, practice your surfing skills on the Flowrider, and experience zero gravity on the Yellowjacket Drop, among multiple adrenaline-pumping options. Or chill in a cabana with a margarita and nachos if that’s more your speed.

Great Wolf Lodge, Grapevine

Part vacation destination resort, part indoor water park, Great Wolf Lodge brings families together throughout the year with several soaking-wet sensations. Fort McKenzie is a four-story treehouse with lots of activities; Slap Tail Pond lets you float in the swells of the wave pool; River Canyon Run puts several people in one tube for its curvy water slide, and Howlin’ Tornado puts you on a six-story drop into the refreshing water below. An adults-only hot springs and seasonal outdoor pool add to the appeal.

Big Bend National Park

From fascinating park plaques with information about history and geography to campsites in hidden canyons, Big Bend National Park in far West Texas is craggy must-see country. With more than 800,000 acres to explore, make sure this park is on your bucket list. A paddle down Santa Elena Canyon, with its easy put ins and beautiful, dramatic canyon views, remains a favorite trip — rangers can direct you to qualified outfitters — while day hikes along Ross Maxwell Scenic drive offer a chance to see native wildlife and historic and cultural sites. Relax and unwind from the day’s activities at the Chisos Mountains Lodge, where you’ll still be surrounded by stunning wilderness vistas full of craggy rocks and native vegetation, even as you enjoy the comforting accommodations.

East Texas

Known as the Piney Woods region for its majestic towering trees, East Texas is a family-friendly destination that captivates visitors with its natural beauty and outdoor adventures. The Big Thicket National Preserve offers fun for all ages with mountain biking trails, canoeing, kayaking, and fishing among the massive cypress trees. Explore the charming city of Nacogdoches, the oldest town in Texas, or hop aboard the historic Texas State Railroad, where steam engines take passengers on a nostalgic four-hour, round-trip journey between Palestine and Rusk. Springtime brings vibrant blooms and family-friendly festivals, including the stunning Tyler Azalea Trail, the Jasper Azalea Festival, and the celebration of the lacy white dogwood trees in Palestine, making it the perfect season to visit.
